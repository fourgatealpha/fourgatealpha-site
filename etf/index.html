What these results mean

The long-run series starts in 1995 and uses proxy data before the relevant ETFs existed. Updated metrics splice subsequent model returns onto the preserved series. Sharpe ratios use the runner’s daily money-market/T-bill proxy. Trading costs and the frozen strategy assumptions are included in the underlying model.

“Time below 10% drawdown” is the share of trading days more than 10% below a previous peak. Recent-period returns are total returns, not annualized returns. Data revisions, execution differences, cash, and share rounding can change real outcomes.

THE PROCESS

Four dimensions.
One weekly allocation.

A systematic framework for equity exposure and a defensive reserve.

Read the market

The framework evaluates price regime, slow momentum, trend quality, and volume pressure. QQQ trend helps inform the equity allocation. The resulting state determines the new sleeve’s equity budget and target mix.

Adapt the mix

The available assets include SPY and QQQ for equity exposure, along with SHY, TLT, UUP, GLD, and cash for defensive allocations. Not every asset is held in every signal.

Stagger the decisions

The portfolio uses three sleeves. One rotates each week, normally holding its cohort for three weeks. A Friday signal applies to the next market session; holidays can shift that date.

Keep the record dated

Each published signal shows its observation date, intended trade date, sleeve, market state, and weights. Signal history and historical performance are presented separately.
